APPLY

Who can participate in the Festival? 

All graduating students on 15-month courses are eligible to participate if their work is complete or at a stage where it can be safely and confidently presented to the public. You can submit your project as a performance, talk, time-based media, interactive desktop or physical exhibit. Space is limited, but the Festival team will do their best to include all students whose work meets the criteria and to ensure participation is meaningful for everyone involved.

What formats are allowed?

  1. Performance 
    A performance is a live presentation that takes place at a specific time during the Festival. It can involve sound, movement, projection, spoken word, or any other time-based form. Performances must be rehearsed and tested in advance, with a clear technical setup and timing plan. You will need to be present for both rehearsals and the live performance.


  2. Talks and Contestations 
    Talks and contestations are short, 15-minute live sessions where you discuss your project in conversation with another student participating in the Festival. You might share the story behind your work, reflect on your process, or question an idea or issue that your project engages with. These sessions are conversational and reflective, meant for dialogue, questions, and exchange. 


  3. Time-based media: video, audio or other linear or looping file  
    Time-based media includes works such as video, sound, or other linear or looping pieces that unfold over time e.g.; short films, recorded performances, video posters, or sound-based works. You should provide the final file, with any notes on duration, looping, and technical setup. Consider the viewing or listening experience and how visitors will encounter your work in the exhibition space.  


  4. Interactive desktop
    Interactive desktop works are digital projects that require direct interaction through standard computer input e.g.: video games, browser-based experiences or other applications. Provide your work as standalone application with clear running instructions and clear technical requirements (software etc.). Multiple projects may share a single setup, rotating throughout the Festival.     


  5. Physical Exhibit 
    Physical exhibits are material installations that require a build or spatial setup in the gallery space. This includes research-based visual displays, photographs, design artefacts, spatial or immersive setups, kinetic experiences, robots, prototypes, or physical interfaces e.g. works that visitors can see, touch, or move around. This category is for tangible, material projects, not software or apps. Think carefully about how visitors will engage with your work in the space. Include notes on space requirements, equipment, and accessibility needs in your application. 

Will my work be provided with all the technical equipment it needs? 

No. Technical support and equipment will be allocated based on available resources and the requirements of all participating projects. The Festival team and technical staff will review requests to ensure fair distribution and safety across the exhibition. Projects with simpler technical needs are generally easier to accommodate. We will review the essential equipment you list in your application form to assess feasibility and plan support in advance.

How do I apply? 

Click on the 'APPLY' link above. You will be directed to a form where you'll need to describe your project and indicate your preferred participation format. You can also select a second or third format option, which helps us determine how your work might best fit within the available space.

Can I participate with work that is not my final MA/MSc project? 

Yes. 

Can I submit more than one work? 

Please choose the project that best represents your academic work and creative practice. We can only accept one submission per student to ensure that space and time are shared fairly across all participants. If you are unsure which work to submit, please discuss it with your course leader before completing your application.

What makes a strong application for the festival? 

The selecting panel looks for clear, concise, realistic plans and complete projects. Explain what visitors will see, hear, or experience, and what resources you need, such as space and equipment.

How will the selection be made? 

An independent panel of CCI technical staff and lecturers will review all submissions without access to student names or course details. The panel will consider how each proposal fits within the overall Festival, including space, diversity of media and themes, accessibility, and technical feasibility.

What criteria will the selection panel use to evaluate applications?

The panel will assess applications based on the following criteria:

  1. Depth of critical analysis + Originality of concept: Does the work demonstrate thoughtful inquiry and present a fresh or innovative approach?


  2. Quality of execution + Level of completion: Is the work well-crafted and developed to a stage where it can be confidently presented to the public?


  3. Clarity of communication + Public engagement potential: Will visitors be able to understand and meaningfully engage with the work?


  4. Space efficiency + Technical feasibility: Can the work be realistically accommodated within the Festival's resources, space, and technical capabilities?

What happens after selection? 

If you apply to take part in the Festival, you must be available on site during both the installation period and the Festival itself. All selected participants, including those presenting talks, videos, or other media-based works, are expected to be present for setup and rehearsals during installation, and to prepare, present, and support their work throughout the Festival. 

Is there any recognition for the participating projects? 

The Winter Festival features a series of awards that recognise creativity, impact, and innovation across CCI. The awards are selected by our extended community of creative practitioners, civic-tech collaborators, and researchers who will visit the Festival and vote for the projects that stand out to them. Award recipients will be announced during the closing celebration. These awards celebrate the diversity of practice within our community and highlight projects that demonstrate excellence across the six categories listed above.

Does my participation in the festival affect my grade? 

The Festival is a public celebration and is not assessed as part of your final major project or any other academic work. Participation is voluntary and offers an opportunity to share the work that best represents your creative and academic journey at CCI. It has no impact on your grade.
